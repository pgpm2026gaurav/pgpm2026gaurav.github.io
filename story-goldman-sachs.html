01 / My experience

When the numbers did not add up.

I joined Goldman Sachs in 2019, working in equity derivatives settlements. I was building tools to help teams reconcile records, identify risk, and make their daily work more reliable.

One of those controls compared independent transaction logs with system records. During a review, I noticed an anomaly and raised concerns internally. It was uncomfortable to challenge what I was seeing so early in my career, but I kept pursuing the discrepancy with senior stakeholders.

The investigation uncovered fraudulent transactions totalling $5.4 million. Recovery required coordinated action across the firm, the banks, and law enforcement. My contribution was noticing the issue, escalating it, and helping the investigation move forward.

Contemporary reporting independently names me as the employee who raised the query. Mint also reported that the entire amount was returned.

The description of the control and my experience is my account. The reporting below documents the incident, escalation, and recovery.

02 / The unexpected part

And then the CEO called.

David Solomon. Tomorrowland. An invitation I did not see coming.

I received a call from Goldman Sachs CEO David Solomon, with an invitation to join him at Tomorrowland. It was a surreal crossover between my working life and my love of upbeat music and spontaneous experiences.

My career also brought recognition through an individual Risk Management Award, a team runner-up in the same category, and later a Regional Client Service Award. The call added a very human, unexpected memory to that journey.

A personal recollection of the call and invitation, rather than a claim that I attended the festival. The articles below do not document this conversation.

05 / What stayed with me

A habit I still build around.

A system is only useful if people can question what it tells them. That is the connection between my work in financial controls and the AI products I build today: preserve the evidence, investigate anomalies, and make the reasoning visible.

My later work at Goldman Sachs expanded into analytics delivery, engineering modernisation, and leading teams. The setting changed, but that habit stayed.
