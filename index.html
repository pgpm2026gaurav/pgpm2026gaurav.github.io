Impact at
Goldman Sachs.

Before building my own AI products, I spent six years working across financial operations, analytics, engineering, and automation. One moment early in that journey shaped how I think about all of them.

September 2019 · Bengaluru

Two transactions.
A $5.4 million consequence.

Early in my career at Goldman Sachs, I spotted transactions that did not add up and raised the concern internally. The escalation helped uncover a $5.4 million fraud. The funds were subsequently recovered through coordinated action by the firm, banks, and authorities.

The technical control mattered. So did being willing to ask an uncomfortable question as a junior employee. That experience still informs how I build: look closely, investigate anomalies, and make it possible for someone to challenge the result.

Method

Speed is a tool.
Thinking is the work.

“Vibe coding” is powerful when it accelerates feedback. It becomes dangerous when fast output is mistaken for understanding. My workflow keeps momentum while making the important assumptions inspectable.

01 / Frame

Find the real decision

Define the user, the job, and the claim the system must eventually earn.

02 / Model

Make thinking explicit

Use prompts, constraints, and system boundaries to turn ambiguity into a testable design.

03 / Build

Create the smallest real loop

Ship the core behavior early enough that reality—not imagination—can shape the next step.

04 / Challenge

Separate proof from promise

Test failure modes and label what is verified, inferred, proposed, or still unknown.

The person behind the processI learn by doing, stay curious about unfamiliar people and domains, and turn an overthinking mind into edge-case planning. The aim is not just better software; it is better judgment.
